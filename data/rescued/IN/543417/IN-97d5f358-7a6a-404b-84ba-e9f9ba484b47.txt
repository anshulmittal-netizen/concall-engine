80% of our total revenue. With Sojern, Adara, Demand Booster, and SoHo now brought together

going to give you more visibility into it by breaking it into three lines of businesses within

Let me begin with properties. Properties includes our hotel marketing and brand engagement 
offerings, including SoHo. Our proposition for hotels is to drive direct bookings and improve 
their marketing ROI. Because much of this business is outcome-based, we grow as our hotels 
grow, and new property additions on our commission model continue through the quarter. Our 
bidding models trained on our travel intent data decide in real time where and when to reach a 
traveler, and that is what turns intent into direct bookings for our hotels.

Properties grew north of 20% year-on-year in H1 and contribute to 44.7% of our MarTech 
revenue. It remains our lead product for entering a new customer relationship, and from there, 
we can expand into our UNO direct stack, helping hotels capture more of that demand 
themselves. Let me now talk about destinations. 
Our destinations business also grew north of 20% organically year-on-year in H1 and now 
accounts for 39.7% of our MarTech revenue. That growth came from our key accounts in the 
North American market and from co-op programs, which remains a focus as we scale them. The 
same AI-driven bidding models that serve our hotels help destinations identify the travelers most 
likely to visit, and our reporting shows them what that spend delivered. For DMOs, measuring 
economic impact now ranks as the leading strategic priority, and that is where our capabilities 
are setting us apart. Destination marketing also follows the travel calendar. DMOs concentrate 
their spend in the months ahead of peak travel season, and many begin new budget cycles in 
July, which makes the July to September quarter a seasonally softer one for this business. 
Visitors also spend on what they do once they arrive, and this quarter, we launched commission 
for experiences in beta, a performance-based marketing solution built for the attractions industry, 
similar to the one that we have for properties. Many attractions are small and medium-sized 
businesses that want to pay only for results, and as that model gains traction, it opens a large and 
fast-growing adjacency for us. 
Our corporate business, which contributes 15.6% of our MarTech revenue, declined about 14% 
organically year on year in H1. I want to be direct with you about why. Historically, corporate 
was not a priority within Sojern, and since the acquisition, our focus has been on integrating 
properties and destinations where we have the most momentum. We are now turning our 
attention to corporate, changing its leadership, and strengthening the team around it. 
We are acting on three fronts. First, we are sharpening our go-to-market by organizing our 
corporate sales teams by vertical, so each team goes deeper on airlines, cruise, car rental, or 
travel brands. Second, we are expanding the channels we offer advertisers into higher growth 
areas, including TikTok, where our agreement is now signed, and connected TV. Third, we are 
adding more data products to the offering, which deepens our role with each customer and makes 
the relationships stickier over time. 
We are already seeing early traction with wins this quarter, including the European division of 
a leading financial services firm, a leading cruise line, one of the largest experiences booking 
platform, one of the largest vacation rental marketplaces. Along with this, we are also expanding 
into new verticals such as cruise lines, where we see good potential. Our airline and car rental 
customers are a natural fit for AI-driven performance marketing built on our travel intent data, 
and we are taking those solutions to them. We expect corporate to be broadly flat in the second 
half after the decline in the first and to build from there.
