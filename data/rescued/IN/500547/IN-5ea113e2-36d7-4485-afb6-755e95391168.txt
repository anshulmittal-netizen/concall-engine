Q2 FY27




<p>Ladies and gentlemen, good day, and welcome to Bharat Petroleum Corporation Limited Q2 FY27 Earnings Conference Call hosted by Antique Stock Broking Limited. As a reminder, all participant lines will be in the listen-only mode and there will be an opportunity for you to ask questions after the presentation concludes. Should you need assistance during the conference call, please signal an operator by pressing star then zero on your touchstone phone. Please note, this conference is being recorded.</p>

<p>I now hand the conference over to Mr. Varatharajan. Thank you, and over to you, sir.</p>

<h3 class="underline_top">Varatharajan Sivasanka:</h3>

<p>Thank you, Naznin. Very good morning, ladies and gentlemen. It&#39;s my pleasure to welcome all the participants on the call and the BPCL management to the 2Q FY27 call of BPCL. We have with us Mr. V.R.K. Gupta, Director of Finance; Mr. Pankaj Kumar, ED, Corporate Finance; Mr. Ashish Goyal, CGM, Corporate Treasury; Ms. Anuya Vatsal Thakar, DGM Finance; Mr. Balagirish, Senior Manager, Finance, Bharat Petroleum Corporation.</p>

<p>I would like to hand over to Mr. Balagirish for a disclaimer.</p>

<h3 class="underline_top">Balagirish:</h3>

<p>Thank you, Mr. Varatharajan. Good morning, and on behalf of the BPCL team, I welcome you all to the post Q2 results con call. Before we begin, I would like to mention that some of the statements that you would be making during this con call are based on our assessment of the matter, and we believe that these statements are reasonable.</p>

<p>However, they involve a number of risks and uncertainties that may lead to different results. Since this is a quarterly result review, please restrict your questions for the Q2 results.</p>

<p>I now request our Director of Finance, Mr. V.R.K. Gupta, who is leading the BPCL team for this call, to make his opening remarks. Thank you, and over to you, sir.</p>


<p>Good morning, everyone, and thank you for joining us today. As you are aware, our quarter 2 results were announced on Friday. Let me begin by putting the quarter in context, particularly how the operating environment evolved since we last spoke. When we met in July, the energy markets were under significant stress due to the escalation of the West Asia crisis, which had a considerable impact on our quarter 1 performance.</p>

<p>As tensions showed some signs of easing at the time, we were hopeful that the operating environment would gradually stabilize. However, quarter 2 unfolded quite differently. The quarter began on a relatively calm note with Brent crude at around $70 per barrel from mid-July.</p>

<p>However, geopolitical tension intensified, pushing crude prices above $90 per barrel by August. The situation escalated further in September following attacks on Saudi export infrastructure and tankers with Brent averaging around $116 per barrel during this month.</p>

<p>Tensions around the Strait of Hormuz and the Red Sea also remained elevated. While we continue to secure supplies without disruption, the escalation kept crude premiums and freight costs at elevated levels. Against this challenging backdrop, strong diesel and ATF cracks supported refining economics, while lower LPG under recoveries provided some relief to marketing margin during the quarter.</p>

<p>At the same time, the rupee remained under pressure, averaging around INR95.6 against U.S. dollar, adding to the cost of our dollar-denominated imports. Through all this volatility, our priorities remained clear. We stayed focused on our 4 things, securing supplies, serving our customers without disruption, protecting shareholder value and maintaining momentum on our growth program.</p>

<p>Let me start with sourcing. Our spot crude share remained at around 67% in Q2, while Russian grades accounted for approximately 37% of our total crude procurement, both broadly in line with previous quarter.</p>

<p>As flows from Middle East improved, we progressively restored sourcing from the region while retaining the flexibility and diversification we had built during Q1. I&#39;m also pleased to share that our efforts to strengthen our international crude sourcing capabilities have begun to yield results.</p>

<p>BPCL became the first in the industry to leverage sourcing opportunities from inside the Strait of Hormuz at a price advantage. This was achieved through our international crude desk in Singapore, which commenced operations in September 2026.</p>

<p>This marks an important step in strengthening our global sourcing capabilities and improving our ability to respond quickly to opportunities in an increasingly dynamic crude market. As of 30th September, we held around 22 days of crude inventory and importantly, our crude sourcing requirements are tied up through December 2026.</p>

<p>Moving to LPG. Despite disruption to traditional supply routes, we maintained domestic availability through a more diversified import portfolio. Supplies from the Arab Gulf improved during the quarter, while U.S. origin cargoes accounted for around 48% of our total LPG imports in Q2.</p>

<p>Currently, we are holding 32 days of LPG inventory, including in transit. On LNG, disruptions to our long-term supply contracts required us to actively diversify our sourcing. We secured alternate spot supplies from the U.S., Oman and West Africa, supported by flexible logistics arrangement to bridge supply gaps despite elevated market prices.</p>

<p>Coming to refining. Our refineries continue to deliver a superlative operational performance during the quarter. We achieved a throughput of 10.4 million metric ton in Q2 with overall capacity utilization at 117%. Our gross refining margin stood at $32.53 per barrel. And after adjusting SAED, the GRM was around $14 per barrel. Distillate yield remained strong at 84.38%.</p>

<p>Moving to marketing. Our domestic sales volume stood at 13.47 million metric ton in Q2 compared to 13.62 MMT in Q1. We continue to strengthen our retail network, taking the number of outlets to 25,592 compared with 25,485 at the end of Q1. At the same time, we continue to build our premium fuel and nonfuel portfolio.</p>

<p>Speed 97 conversion stood at 5.21% during the quarter. Speed 100 is now available at 162 outlets, and our BeCafe network has expanded to 234 locations.</p>

<p>On LPG, we commissioned our Matia LPG plant in Goalpara, Assam, our first LPG bottling plant in the state with a capacity of 30 TMT per annum. This is an important step towards strengthening our LPG infrastructure and product availability in the region. We are also pleased to share that our premium composite cylinder, Bharatgas Lite ZIP, is now available across India and can be conveniently booked through the Hello BPCL app.</p>

<p>I&#39;m proud to share with you that BPCL has recently launched extreme weather grade diesel developed entirely in-house to keep military vehicles operating reliably in extreme winters and at high altitudes. The first rake was dispatched from Bina in August. For us, this is more than just a new product. It demonstrates what we can achieve when innovation and technological capabilities come together in support of national priorities.</p>

<p>Our gas business maintained strong growth momentum. Bulk Gas achieved record H1 sales performance of 979 TMT, registering 53% year-on-year growth, while CGD sales reached 177 TMT, growing 65% year-on-year.</p>

<p>The recently announced GOBARdhan scheme has made CBG projects increasingly attractive, and we are actively pursuing this opportunity through both our own plants and joint ventures. Additionally, our CBG blending reached 6.5% in H1, more than double the mandated target of 3%.</p>

<p>Coming to LPG compensation, the softening of Saudi CP prices during Q2 provided some relief on under recoveries. Average under recovery declined to around INR290 per cylinder in Q2 compared to INR507 per cylinder in Q1.</p>

<p>As of 30th September, the cumulative buffer stood at INR17,334 crores after adjusting for INR3,796 crores of compensation received during the first half. For future, we remain hopeful of the government&#39;s continued support to OMCs towards domestic LPG compensation.</p>

<p>Let me now brief on our growth program. Our capex program remains on track. And since we last spoke, our key projects have continued to make steady progress. As of September &#39;26, our Bina Petrochemicals and refinery expansion project has reached 40.2% progress, while the polypropylene project at Kochi has reached 55%. The PRFCC project at Mumbai is also progressing.</p>

<p>We have also taken an important step in our sustainable aviation fuel journey. In October, we signed India&#39;s first co-processing licensing agreement for our SAF project at Mumbai refinery. Phase 1 involves revamping the existing Kerosene hydrotreater unit and is targeted for commissioning by January 2027. This positions us well for India&#39;s SAF branding requirement for international flights, beginning at 1% in 2027 and progressively increasing to 5% by 2030.</p>

<p>On the Andhra Pradesh refinery and petrochemical complex, I&#39;m pleased to share that on Friday, our Board approved the incorporation of a wholly owned subsidiary as a dedicated project company with an initial authorized share capital of up to INR4,900 crores, subject to requisite approvals. The dedicated project company will facilitate swift due diligence, lender engagement and development of the financing and partnership structures.</p>

<p>The detailed feasibility reports are in the final stages, following which the financial investment decision will be taken shortly. Moving to our upstream portfolio. The Mozambique project continues to progress steadily, reaching 47% completion as of September 2026, with gas production expected by mid-2028.</p>

<p>In Brazil, following the award of the FPSO tender in May 2026, the BM-SEAL-11 project is also progressing in line with planned time lines. Overall, against our FY26-&#39;27 capex target of INR25,000 crores, we have incurred INR13,749 crores during the first half, including INR9,300 crores in Q2.</p>

<p>Now coming to our financial performance. Revenue from operations for the quarter stood at INR1.5 lakh crores with a stand-alone profit after tax of INR4,993 crores. For the first half, stand-alone profit after tax stood at INR1,031 crores. On a consolidated basis, profit after tax for Q2 stood at INR4,863 crores, while profit for the first half was INR2,990 crores.</p>

<p>Despite the challenging operating environment, our Q2 performance demonstrates the resilience of our integrated business model. The quarter benefited from our agile sourcing strategy, robust refining performance and a reduction in marketing and LPG under recoveries. Our balance sheet also remains strong. Stand-alone gross borrowings reduced to INR15,246 crores as of 30th September compared with INR17,386 crores at the end of June.</p>

<p>The stand-alone debt-to-equity ratio improved to 0.16 from 0.19 in Q1. Our borrowings, net of short-term investments stood at INR7,106 crores as against INR4,800 crores in Q1. As we enter the second half of the year, the external environment continues to remain uncertain.</p>

<p>However, our performance in Q2 demonstrates our ability to adapt quickly and operate with resilience through periods of significant turbulence. We remain confident in BPCL&#39;s ability to navigate near-term volatility while building long-term value.</p>

<p>With that, I will conclude my brief on our Q2 performance, and we&#39;ll be happy to take your questions. Thank you.</p>


<p>Thank you very much. We will now begin with the question-and-answer session. The first question is from line of Probal Sen from ICICI Securities.</p>


<p>Congratulations on a good set of numbers. A few questions quickly. You talked about the crude sourcing mix for the second quarter, with spot crude at 67%-odd and Russia at 37%. Just wanted to get a sense, given that Middle East crude flows have improved, as you mentioned, have we seen any material change in that mix in the month of October so far? And how are we looking at the mix till December when you said you already have crude tied up till at least the end of December?</p>


<p>Let me give in terms of the brief numbers. The Saudi side, Persian Gulf compared to Q1, definitely, it has improved in Q2. Even October, November also, we are expecting a similar lines of higher volume from Middle East, mainly after Yanbu pipeline correction by Saudi, they have started increasing their volumes and certain cargoes are coming from ADNOC also.</p>

<p>Only cargoes from Iraq and Kuwait still not coming in full fledged. But otherwise, a good amount of volume is coming from Middle Eastern side. October, our requirement from Russia has slightly come down. But in the month of November, the cargoes from Russia significantly has reduced due to the commercial viability of Russian cargoes, mainly the increase in the freight, we are not getting good offers from the Russia.</p>

<p>Now for December cargoes, slightly it has improved, whatever they were expecting a good amount of premium for Russian cargoes. Now the premiums slightly, they have reduced. Maybe it is now comparable with other Persian Gulf grades, Russian Urals. Maybe for December, some couple of cargoes we can take Russian. November will be definitely low in terms of Russian availability -- not availability, Russian cargoes, what we have procured.</p>


<p>Right, sir. So just a follow-up on that. If I remember correctly, sir, I think you had mentioned at the Q1 briefing that blended crude cost was somewhere around Indian benchmark plus/minus about $12 to $15. Has that number changed in terms of delivered crude price to us in terms of actual cost accrued?</p>


<p>Still the freight rates are high, the insurance rate is slightly higher. Still benchmark plus $10 to $12, it depends on which side you are taking the product. For example, if it is from Fujairah, definitely it will be lesser. But if it is for from Yanbu, the freight rates should be higher. But from benchmark, we can take on an average, the landing level is $10 to $12.</p>


<p>Right, sir. The second question was with respect to the LPG. Now sir, obviously, you mentioned, I think, INR290-odd a cylinder for Q2. Now we have seen Asia LPG prices spike quite a bit in October. So if we can get a sense month-wise, what was the per cylinder under recovery in October? And what was it in September? And what are you looking at in November at this point of time?</p>


<p>See, if you see the numbers, August -- July, it was INR490, August, INR179, September INR199. We are expecting October will be INR245, and it is going up in the month of November, November, expecting at INR328 per cylinder.</p>


<p>Okay. Okay. And one last question, sir, if I may. In terms of the marketing margins, is there any discussion at all in terms of looking -- relooking at the pricing given how sharply product spreads and prices have increased. I know you can&#39;t answer in terms of time line.</p>

<p>But just wanted your sense on how you&#39;re looking to navigate the stronger retail -- I mean, higher retail marketing losses, which I&#39;m sure you are encountering at least in October. How are you looking at the situation?</p>


<p>If you see the quarter 2 numbers, integrated side, there is no big stress on the balance sheet and profitability. We are almost at a normal level of profitability for the quarter, slightly on the lower side, mainly for the higher losses in the marketing under recoveries.</p>

<p>But we thought crude prices will stabilize, but still the war is continuing. There is no clarity by what time actually things will improve. But one thing we are sure, once this war resolves, crude cannot command at this price because the supplies are there, supplies are there, production is there.</p>

<p>Only thing the route is the disturbing thing. And this Red Sea side, volumes are coming, Yanbu side, even there are certain Houthi attacks on Saudi side, but still a good amount of volumes are coming from the Red Sea.</p>

<p>But once SOH route is resolved, we are hopeful crude can come back to the reasonable level. But some more time, we have to wait and see. We are reviewing every month-on-month what is the under recoveries, how long this will continue. But there is no further discussions on any improvement on the marketing margins at this point of time.</p>


<p>Next question is from the line of Vivekanand from Ambit Private Limited.</p>


<p>Just 2 questions. Number one is on the Mozambique asset. You said that the construction is, I think, 40%, I guess, is what -- 47% is what you said. Can you refresh us on the targets that are there in terms of when -- by when should this project come on stream and first gas? And what will be your share of gas in, let&#39;s say, FY30 from this asset? That&#39;s number one.</p>

<p>The second one is in terms of your capex outlook. Any change versus the guidance given? I believe it was around INR25,000 crores in the past. Any change on the annual target for FY27 and &#39;28, along with maybe a key update on the Bina expansion, if you could talk about that.</p>


<p>Yes. Let me explain on Mozambique first because the project is progressing very well. 47% project progress has achieved. We are expecting mid of 2028, either June or July, the commission date. They have declared at June &#39;28, the first gas we can see. As of date, there is no change in the schedule date.</p>

<p>So based on the current progress of the project, we are hopeful mid of &#39;28, we can see the gas. That is one. In terms of the gas flows, size of the gas, the first phase development is for 13 million metric ton of 2 trains development, out of which BPCL is having a 10% share. That means our share-out will be around 1.3 million metric ton of gas. This is only Phase 1 development.</p>

<p>And subsequently, based on the reserves, we can develop 2 more trains. Once this particular project is completed, then other trains, we can plan it. So the total size will be around -- BPCL share will be -- BPRL share will be around 4 -- roughly 4.2 million to 4.5 million metric ton of gas if all 3 trains are developed based on the reserves.</p>

<p>And the second question, in terms of the capex program, our financial year FY25-&#39;26, the target -- &#39;26-&#39;27, the target is INR25,000 crores. In the 6 months period, we have already reached around INR13,000 crores. We are keeping the same target.</p>

<p>But in case if any good progress on the project side, slightly it may go up beyond INR25,000 crores. It depends on the project progress, even certain activities, if the progress is good, maybe this capex program can go up slightly.</p>


<p>Just one follow-up. Any plans on tying up the Mozambique gas to get to Indian customers given that now you have visibility of completion of the project by mid of &#39;28?</p>


<p>Mozambique, we have already tied up SPA for 1 million metric ton of gas to bring it to India. Out of 1 million metric ton already we have started marketing in Q2. Q2 itself, we have started marketing. And certain customers already we have tied up, but small volumes. We have started marketing of the gases.</p>


<p>Right. By when can we expect this gas for you to get tied up, the whole 1 MMTPA? And do you have plans to bring the entire 1.3 MMTPA?</p>


<p>We have started in Q2 on the sales side. On the sales side, we have started marketing of this gas in Q2. Maybe in next 2, 3 quarters, we can complete 1 MMT.</p>


<p>Next question is from the line of Sarthak from DSP Asset Managers.</p>

<h3 class="underline_top">Sarthak:</h3>

<p>Sir, just a couple of questions on the Mozambique side as well. So if we see the balance sheet on the intangibles under development, can you please split as to how much of the INR19,000-odd crores intangibles as well as under the equity method of INR29,000 crores would be pertaining to Mozambique? And how much have we spent post the force majeure? And what can we expect as an incremental spending on the Mozambique side?</p>


<p>Yes. Mozambique as of date, cumulative basis, we have spent around $2.8 billion. It&#39;s almost around INR27,461 crores. And the future capex commitments based on the current approved project cost is $1.8 billion in the next coming couple of years, we have to spend. The total investment in this Mozambique project will become $2.8 billion plus $1.8 billion around $4.5 billion, $4.6 billion of investment, including the exploration activity, everything.</p>

<h3 class="underline_top">Sarthak:</h3>

<p>Okay. And this will be up till the gas will be started. So that is till 2028, right?</p>


<p>&#39;28, mid of &#39;28.</p>

<h3 class="underline_top">Sarthak:</h3>

<p>Okay. And how much incremental interest cost is there? Has there any uptick on the interest cost for this project?</p>


<p>For this project, this project development is happening under the project cost -- project financing. There are a consortium of around 8 or 9 bankers consortium at project level. The interest cost will be varying actually. It starts from 6% to 8.5%, 9% based on the current interest rate. So different lenders have different terms and conditions, this project financing has been concluded.</p>

<h3 class="underline_top">Sarthak:</h3>

<p>Fair. This was very clear, sir. And one last thing. The delta that we see in the balance sheet of intangibles, that is mostly pertaining to Mozambique. Is my understanding correct? So INR16,000 crores has increased to INR19,700 crores, the intangibles under development and a similar increase has been there on the investment under equity method as well?</p>


<p>Yes, investment under equity method is mainly, mainly due to, the particular project, Mozambique project. Earlier it was under PI holding. PI holding means whatever capex is straight away due to common under capex program.</p>

<p>But subsequently in the last quarter, last 2 quarters, there was a development. All the assets in development has been moved to Asset Co. That means instead of capex, you will see in the investments. At project level, all the assets and the work in progress moved to equity under Asset Co. So that this capex program, whatever incurred a capex program under development, it has moved to equity.</p>


<p>Next question is from the line of Yogesh Patil from Dolat Capital.</p>


<p>Congrats for the good set of numbers. BPCL GRM has dropped the quarter-on-quarter closer to $32 per barrel compared to the Q1. What&#39;s driving this decline in the BPCL GRM, because most of the independent refiners actually saw their GRM jump on a sequential basis. Could you walk us through what&#39;s behind this quarter-on-quarter dip in our reported GRM? And was there any crude inventory gains during Q2?</p>


<p>Crude inventory gain, generally, we don&#39;t calculate because our average inventory is around 22 to 25 days. So there won&#39;t be any big impact on crude inventories. If any inventory gains are there. To the extent of intermediates, if you are holding a very big amount of intermediaries, then there may be an impact.</p>

<p>Otherwise, compared to Q1, the GRM reduction is mainly 2 reasons. One is slightly the -- more or less GRMs are in the same level, Q1 and Q2. Q1 average gas oil spreads are $55, now it is $56 in Q2. Gasoline also $24 and $24. There is no big change in the spread, but 2 important things have happened in the crude procurement.</p>

<p>One is the crude freight costs have significantly increased compared to Q1. The Q2 freight costs are very high. And second is Q1, at least most of the period, we used to get Russian crude either at benchmark discount or around the benchmark.</p>

<p>But in Q2, certain cargoes we don&#39;t get any discounts on the Russian crude, we have to buy either at the premium of benchmarks or benchmarks. So these 2 have impacted, mostly on the high sulfur grades, impacted the refining GRMs compared to Q1. Otherwise, if you compare the Singapore benchmark GRMs, still our GRMs are much higher.</p>


<p>But, sir, the freight cost has gone up for every independent refiner. So why, in our case, crude cost is mattering to the GRM calculation?</p>


<p>I don&#39;t know other industries, what is the impact of crude inventory gain or intermediary inventory gain, what is the carry forward from Q1 to Q2, we don&#39;t have any such information. We generally track what is your performance compared to the trend of the spread, what is the crude procurement compared to the market trends. As long as we are better than the market trends, that we can conclude our refinery performance is better.</p>

<p>Secondly, regarding LPG losses, Q3 to Q4 losses are not that significant, around INR 3,100&ndash;3,200 crores for both quarters, even though contract prices went up seasonally. Did the company take any steps to optimize LPG sales volumes or restrict losses?</p>


<p>No, it follows a similar trend. Losses depend primarily on sales volumes. The formula remains the same.</p>


<p>One of your peers reported much higher Q-o-Q LPG losses, around 20&ndash;25%. Why the difference?</p>


<p>It depends mainly on volumes. I cannot comment on others&rsquo; volumes, but the pricing formula (CP-based) is standard across the industry. Sale price and landing cost methodology are the same for all companies.</p>

<h3 class="underline_top">Rahul Agrawal:</h3>

<p>For Sabri&rsquo;s reference, the CP has been around INR 620&ndash;630 over the last six months.</p>


<p>And there is no big window for LPG optimization. We are not doing any major optimization beyond the usual measures.</p>


<p>Got it. Given that oil has corrected to $60, we have seen LPG hold up quite strongly. Even the June contract prices are down only about $5. There have been reports that US tariffs and other factors are causing significant readjustments in LPG flows. What is your assessment regarding Arab Gulf LPG versus US LPG, and potential opportunities to reduce your own cost of purchasing LPG and thereby reduce under-recoveries?</p>


<p>Most of our LPG is under term contracts, primarily Arab Gulf-based. However, we are seeing some opportunity with US LPG. We estimate a potential $20&ndash;$30 per metric ton benefit if we can optimize by shifting certain cargoes from AG to US sources. We are exploring this and, wherever feasible, coordinating with suppliers to implement this optimization. This could provide a net benefit of about $20&ndash;$30 per ton, including freight and other costs.</p>


<p>$20&ndash;$30 net benefit, including freight and other costs, correct?</p>


<p>Yes, that is correct.</p>


<p>Ladies and gentlemen, that was the last question for today. I now hand the conference over to Mr. Varatharajan Sivasankara from Antique Stock Broking for closing comments.</p>

<h3 class="underline_top">Varatharajan Sivasankara:</h3>

<p>You mentioned 340 outlets added during the year. Could you rify the net number of your own CGDs and total CNG stations, including other GAs?</p>

<h3 class="underline_top">Rahul Agrawal:</h3>

<p>As of 31st March 2025, we have a total of 2,370 CNG stations. About 840 are in our own GAs, and approximately 1,530 are in retail outlets under other GAs.</p>

<h3 class="underline_top">Varatharajan Sivasankara:</h3>

<p>The 840 number corresponds to the 20 or so GAs that you operate, correct?</p>


<p>Yes, that is correct.</p>

<h3 class="underline_top">Varatharajan Sivasankara:</h3>

<p>Fair enough. I would like to thank the management and all participants for taking the time to join today&rsquo;s call. It is always a pleasure. Thank you, and have a nice day.</p>



<h3 class="underline_top">Rahul Agrawal:</h3>



<p>On behalf of Antique Stock Broking Limited, this concludes the conference. Thank you for joining, and you may now disconnect your lines.</p>
