 Order book and outlook. During Q2 '27, the order book and demand was affected 
by a couple of factors. First, the polymer prices historically remained at the highest 
levels. At these prices, customers are reducing their inventory levels. 
 Second, the energy crisis in Europe caused by shortage of oil and gas has driven up 
power costs and hit industrial activity, particularly the chemical sector in Germany. 
Europe is our largest market accounting for almost 65% of geographical mix. And 
the weakness in the European demand remains our area of concern going forward. 
 The third factor was the El Nino weather conditions that affected agriculture output 
and also fertilizer demand in agriculture-based economies such as Brazil, reducing 
the demand for bulk packaging in these segments. 
 Fourth, the ocean freight cost remains high and container availability at Kanpur ICD 
was also affected during this period. This raised the landed cost of our products for 
overseas buyers and had led some customers to defer purchases. We continue to issue 
regular market advisories to our customers to enable timely commercial discussions 
and fair pass-through of cost increases. We do see some easing out on this front. 
 Our diversified customer base across Europe, North America, South America and 
markets with long relationships give us resilience during these tough times. Despite 
lower volumes, our focus on realizations and improved product mix helped us to 
continue with the profitability. Customers are buying closer to their immediate 
requirements. 
 Most of our order book continues to be for dispatch within a month, consistent with 
the demand visibility of three to four weeks, as mentioned earlier. We have seen a 
slight dip in the average order quantity during this period. 
 Looking ahead, we expect these conditions to persist in the near term and volumes in 
the quarter may also remain affected. We are monitoring demand extremely closely,
