As you know, the first quarter of this year turned out to be considerably more difficult than we 
had anticipated. European demand remained subdued and a number of customers deferred 
their orders amid geopolitical uncertainty, raw material markets were volatile. Our revenue for 
Q1 FY27 fell to Rs. 14.36 crores. When we announced these results in August, we were 
transparent about the situation and we moderated our early expectations of FY27 being a year 
of non-linear growth. 
Over the past three months, we have worked steadily to meet the challenges. The second 
quarter shows a clear improvement over the first. The recovery is not yet complete and we still 
have a lot to do. 
Let me first take you through the financial performance and then we will share the updates 
from business. For the second quarter of FY27, revenue from operations stood at Rs. 23.39 
crores. This is an increase of 62.62% over the Rs. 14.36 crores last quarter, although it is 23% 
lower than Rs. 30.32 crores in Q2 FY26. EBITDA excluding other income and exceptional items 
stood at Rs. 3.94 crores. This is 17% higher than Rs. 3.35 crores in Q1 FY27 and 59% lower than 
Rs. 9.62 crores in same quarter last year. Our EBITDA margin for the quarter was 16.9% 
compared to 23.3% in previous quarter and 31.7% in Q2 FY26. 
Profit after tax for the quarter was Rs. 1.63 crores, an increase of 46% over Rs. 1.12 crores in 
Q1 FY27 and a decline of 67% from Rs. 4.96 crores in Q2 FY26. Earnings per share for the quarter 
stood at 1.28. 
I would like to spend a moment on the margins since this is the area where the quarter was the 
weakest. Two factors were at work. First, although the volumes picked up over the first quarter, 
they are still below our earlier levels. A meaningful part of our cost is fixed. So, when the 
volumes are lower, the margins come under pressure. Second, raw material prices rose 
significantly during the quarter and remained volatile throughout. Our operating expenses 
increased to Rs. 19.36 crores from Rs. 11.01 crores in Q1, a rise of 76% while the revenue grew 
at 62%. That difference explains why our margin declined even though as an absolute EBITDA 
improved. 
We expect input costs to ease as geopolitical conditions stabilize, our margins to normalize as 
our sales return to earlier levels. Throughout this period, we remained focused on healthy 
profitability and that includes new products that we are developing. Despite a softer first half, 
our balance sheet remained strong. Total borrowings came down to Rs. 25.99 crores from Rs. 
30.67 crores at the end of March, mainly due to the reduction in short-term borrowing from 
Rs. 9.42 crores to Rs. 5.37 crores. As a result, our finance cost for the half reduced to Rs. 0.89 
crores from Rs. 1.95 crores in H1 FY26. 
Our cash and bank balances together with the investments stood at Rs. 28.74 crores, which is 
higher than our total borrowings. We generated Rs. 14.64 crores of cash from operating

activities during the first half compared with Rs. 17.66 crores in H1 FY26. Trade receivables 
reduced to Rs. 21.12 crores from Rs. 32.57 crores at the end of March. The capital work-in- 
progress rose to Rs. 16.6 crores from Rs. 12.38 crores, reflecting our continued investment in 
Block 7B. 
Now, let me turn to the business: 
Much of the improvement in the second quarter came from our core pharmaceutical 
intermediate business. To make up for the softer exports, we sharpened our focus on the 
domestic market and this has helped us to restore volumes. Alongside this, we have begun to 
see improved traction from our European customers. This gives us confidence that the core 
business will recover to its earlier levels over the next few quarters. We have also broadened 
our portfolio during the quarter. We commenced commercial production of the two new 
pharmaceutical intermediates, one for anti-diabetic application and one for cardiovascular 
application. Both products have received an encouraging response in the domestic market and 
we plan to take them to the export market in the coming quarters. 
While we work on this recovery, we continue to build businesses that will drive our next phase 
of growth. Our CRAMS business made strong progress during the quarter. On our specialty 
chemicals projects, the South East Asia market, we have successfully completed the pilot trials 
and the batches are now undergoing the stability testing. We expect the commercial supplies 
to begin from Q1 FY28. For the second specialty product, the same region has cleared 
laboratory trials and has now moved to the pilot stage. In Europe, we are making positive 
progress on two molecules, one for the pharmaceutical applications and one for the 
agrochemical applications. 
As in our previous calls, I would request participants to not ask names for the specific products 
of the customer as these engagements are covered by the confidentiality agreement. We will 
further share the details as and when we are able to. 
To sum up, this year has brought its own set of challenges. We have responded by 
strengthening our domestic presence, adding to our product portfolio and advancing our 
CRAMS pipeline. As we have said before, given the project nature of our business, the long lead 
times involved in our quarterly results can be uneven. However, the direction is clear. The core 
business is recovering, our new products are gaining traction and our CRAMS projects are 
moving closer to the commercial supply. New capacity is coming online. 
With a stronger balance sheet to support this, we look forward to a stronger performance in 
the period ahead.
