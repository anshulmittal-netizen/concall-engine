Rajiv Anand: Good evening, everyone, and thank you for joining us today. I am joined by our 
Executive Directors, Jagdeep Mallareddy and Ganesh Sankaran; our CFO, Viral 
Damania; and our Head of Investor Relations & Strategy, Indrajit Yadav. 
I will start with the operating environment and then cover our progress on the Bank's 
strategic priorities. 
During the quarter, global markets faced renewed geopolitical tensions and higher 
energy prices. India's growth momentum, by contrast, stayed resilient, backed by 
strong economic activity and sound macroeconomic fundamentals. Banking-system 
liquidity improved meaningfully, helped by FCNR(B) deposit flows, and credit demand 
continued to strengthen. Together, these conditions have created a constructive 
environment for the banking sector. 
Against this backdrop, the Bank delivered a healthy operating performance. Growth 
momentum picked up, and we kept a prudent approach to risk and liquidity. We also 
made further progress on our strategic priorities, with better asset quality, a stronger 
balance-sheet mix and higher profitability. These results show the benefits of the 
disciplined actions we have taken over the past several quarters. 
I will now cover the key highlights for Q2FY27, followed by business-specific progress 
and financial performance. 
1. Acceleration in Business Momentum Across Segments: 
• Retail deposits grew sharply during the quarter, helped by FCNR(B) 
mobilisation, and asset growth picked up across both retail and corporate 
segments. As a result, deposits and advances have returned to double-digit 
growth, at 10% and 11% YoY respectively, compared with a decline last year. 
• Under the RBI swap window, we mobilised USD 3.51 billion of FCNR(B) 
deposits. This implies a market share of around 2.75%, well above our 
natural share of around 1.5% in overall deposits. It is also meaningful relative 
to our own size, at 6.3% of our Q2 average deposit book and was supported 
by a relatively higher share of retail and non-leveraged deposits, reinforcing 
the quality of our funding profile. 
• On the asset side, retail growth was robust at 12% QoQ overall and 4% QoQ 
excluding FCNR(B)-related lending. Disbursements accelerated across 
vehicle, rural and traditional retail. The wholesale book kept its momentum, 
growing 10% QoQ, while the portfolios under Mid-market & SME segments 
also witnessed healthy 13% QoQ growth. 
2. Growth achieved along with robust Asset Quality: 
• Asset quality improved across all sub-segments. Annualised gross slippages 
fell to 1.8%, from 2.1% QoQ and 3.0% YoY. Our continued focus on 
recoveries ensured a larger improvement in net slippages, which fell to 1.1%, 
from 1.5% QoQ and 2.5% YoY. 
• The outstanding stress book has declined, with gross and net NPAs 
improving to 2.73% and 0.83% respectively. Residual stress indicators 
remain negligible: net security receipts are at 0.03%, and the SMA 1 and 2 
book combined is at 0.05%.

3. Continued investment in digital and physical infrastructure: 
• We resumed expanding our physical distribution footprint, adding 124 
outlets, primarily within BFIL. With business momentum picking up, we plan 
to add 100+ bank branches in H2, expanding into underpenetrated 
geographies and reaccelerating growth in select markets where expansion 
had previously been moderated. In parallel, we strengthened our frontline 
capacity by adding around 4,300 employees at overall bank level, largely in 
sourcing roles. These investments in distribution reach and sales capacity 
position us well to support future growth. 
• Our generative AI initiatives continued to see strong employee adoption. 
More than 50% of employees now use our AI chatbots, and over half of 
active users use them daily with satisfactory outcomes. AI-assisted 
productivity is steadily becoming part of day-to-day work across the 
organisation. 
• We have also expanded our conversational AI program, with six voice bot 
programs launched and a further twelve customer and employee journeys 
identified within the roadmap. These programs are helping validate use 
cases, user adoption patterns, and operational benefits while creating a 
pipeline for broader deployment. 
4. Financial outcomes moving towards our stated ambition: 
• We had set an ambition of a 1% exit RoA for this year, and we are making 
clear progress. Q2 RoA improved to 0.87%, compared with a normalised 
0.63% in Q1, despite temporary margin pressure from FCNR(B) mobilisation. 
• Pre-provision operating profit rose to Rs.2,874 crore, up 15% on the 
normalised previous quarter. Robust business growth and healthy fee 
income drove the increase, while disciplined balance-sheet management 
contained the margin impact of FCNR(B)-related liquidity. 
• Provisions declined 12% QoQ and 54% YoY, reflecting sustained 
improvement in asset quality and lower fresh stress. 
• As a result, profit after tax rose to Rs.1,242 crore. This is up 51% QoQ on the 
normalised previous quarter and against a loss a year ago. 
• Capital adequacy remains healthy, with CET1 at 15.55% and CRAR at 16.59%, 
giving us ample capacity to support growth. 
I will now take you through the individual businesses, starting with Retail Banking

Retail Banking: 
1. Deposit Franchise: 
• Our deposit franchise is anchored by a wide distribution network, strong 
segment-led propositions, a comprehensive product suite, and a capable 
workforce. 
• Over the last few quarters, we have further strengthened our liability 
franchise through targeted senior and mid-level leadership hires, greater 
integration across branch, affluent and NRI banking teams, and 
enhancements to digital onboarding and acquisition journeys. 
• During the quarter, we mobilised USD 3.51 billion of FCNR(B) deposits under 
the RBI swap scheme. This shows the strength of our NRI franchise and our 
ability to seize market opportunities through sharp execution.

• We have used these inflows prudently to improve the quality of our 
liabilities. Part of the proceeds replaced higher-cost bulk funding, which 
brought Certificates of Deposit down to 3.3% of deposits, from 5.9% QoQ. 
• Our deposit mix continues to improve. On an end-of-period basis, retail 
deposits as defined under LCR grew 11% QoQ and rose to 50% of total 
deposits. 
• Customer acquisition gained momentum, with nearly 3.6 lakh new-to-bank 
accounts opened during the quarter, up 12% QoQ, supported by a stronger 
customer proposition and more productive distribution. 
• Cost of deposits was 5.96%, compared with 5.93% QoQ. While both savings 
and term deposit costs repriced downwards, the marginal increase came 
from a lower CASA mix due to FCNR(B) mobilisation. 
• The FCNR(B) window gave a strong boost to our Affluent and NRI Banking. 
These segments now hold around Rs.1,05,000 crore of deposits, up 23% 
QoQ. We also added SIF offerings to our approved product suite, giving 
clients access to more sophisticated investment solutions and strengthening 
our wealth proposition. 
• We continued to strengthen our customer proposition through selective 
launches and partnerships: 
o A partnership with Navi UPI to strengthen digital payments 
o A partnership with JP Morgan Payments to launch Xpedite, our 
cross-border remittance solution 
o New customer features, Secure Deposit and Safe Buddy on INDIE, 
to enhance security and trust 
• Going forward, we will keep focusing on improving CASA, increasing the 
retail deposit share and optimising the cost of deposits. We will do this 
through deeper engagement with retail, affluent, NRI, senior citizen and 
SME customers, supported by digital, AI and data capabilities. 
 
Moving to retail assets, I will start with Vehicle Finance. 
 
2. Vehicle Finance: 
• Our Vehicle Finance portfolio crossed the Rs.1 lakh crore milestone this 
quarter. The book now stands at Rs.1,00,651 crore, up 5% YoY and 1% QoQ. 
• Disbursements picked up to Rs.11,968 crore, up 17% YoY and 10% QoQ. As 
we said on earlier calls, we have been cautious on tractors and two-
wheelers. Excluding these two segments, vehicle disbursement growth was 
even stronger, at 24% YoY and 14% QoQ. 
• The disbursement growth was driven by both Commercial Vehicles which 
grew 32% YoY and 17% QoQ, and Passenger Vehicle at 13% YoY and 12% 
QoQ. 
• Asset quality remained stable, backed by disciplined underwriting and 
collections. Annualised net slippages fell to 1.67%, down 34 bps QoQ and 30 
bps YoY, and the overdue book stayed within our operating range. 
• Looking ahead, festive demand and seasonality should support growth in the 
second half. We, however, remain watchful of macro-economic trends 
especially around fuel prices, affordability considerations and monsoon-
related developments across markets. 
• Our focus remains on profitable growth, gaining share in our chosen 
segments and deepening customer relationships through cross-sell across 
the wider retail bank.

3. Rural Banking: 
• Our Rural Banking franchise continued to improve across the board, in both 
disbursements and asset quality. 
• The micro-loan business returned to growth after nearly eight quarters of 
decline. The portfolio grew 8% QoQ to Rs.17,571 crore, on disbursements of 
Rs.6,548 crore, up 25% QoQ. 
• Asset quality improved further, reinforcing our confidence in the 
underwriting changes made last year. Annualised net slippages in micro 
loans fell to 2.2%, from 3.4% QoQ and 14.1% YoY. The 31–90 DPD book fell 
to 0.4%, from 0.6% QoQ and 3.2% YoY. 
• Around 89% of the portfolio is now covered under the CGFMU credit 
guarantee, including disbursements whose cover is currently being 
processed. 
• Beyond microfinance, our diversification initiatives continue to scale. The 
Merchant Finance portfolio grew 20% YoY and 7% QoQ to Rs.8,694 crore, 
serving nano retailers across more than 550 cities. Merchant Finance now 
contributes 33% of loans originated by BFIL vs 25% YoY. Affordable Housing 
grew 18% YoY, while KCC & other rural loans remained stable QoQ. 
• Rural Banking remains an important franchise, both for profitability and for 
priority-sector lending. Having strengthened underwriting and operating 
processes in microfinance, we have resumed growth in a calibrated manner 
and expect to build on this momentum as we enter the seasonally stronger 
second half. At the same time, we continue to monitor potential risks from 
drought conditions in a few states, fuel and inflation trends along with next 
state elections, and we will calibrate our growth strategy accordingly. 
 
4. Consumer Loans: 
• Building on the momentum of the first quarter, the traditional Consumer 
Banking assets witnessed further acceleration in growth during the second 
quarter. 
• Excluding FCNR(B)-related loans, the consumer loan book grew 7% QoQ to 
Rs.59,776 crore, led by healthy traction in secured retail and MSME. 
• We continued to scale our secured retail and MSME franchises while 
maintaining a selective approach towards unsecured lending. Our Home 
loan book grew 39% YoY and 12% QoQ to Rs.7,733 crore. 
• Within MSME, Small Business Banking grew 10% QoQ to Rs.10,523 crore. 
Loan Against Property grew 7% QoQ to Rs.14,407 crore. Both reflect 
encouraging traction in our focus segments. 
• In unsecured lending, origination trends improved, but we continued to put 
portfolio quality and risk-adjusted returns ahead of volume. As a result, the 
credit card portfolio declined 3% QoQ and the personal loan portfolio 
declined 2% QoQ. With new originations improving, we expect both CC and 
PL books to return to growth within a few quarters. 
• Asset quality improved further during the quarter, with annualized net 
slippages of 3.3% vs 3.7% YoY and 3.5% QoQ. 
• Over the last several quarters, we have strengthened our leadership team 
and invested in distribution, analytics and technology. That work is now 
showing in better momentum across our focus businesses. Mortgages, gold 
loans, LAP and Small Business Banking are contributing a growing share of 
incremental growth and diversifying the loan book. These businesses are 
central to our strategy of building a more granular, balanced and resilient 
universal bank.
